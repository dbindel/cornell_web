You get two sermons for the price of one this week.  Don’t worry, they’re both
short!

First, I was very happy about Alex Townsend’s comment to you all at last week’s
panel about the role of community in CAM (and in applied mathematics more
broadly).  I’ve said it before, and I’ll say again, I think this community is
something special.  But a big part of the value of any community comes from the
effort that is invested by the members of that community — i.e., you!  A PhD is
not just be about a set of technical skills, but about adoption into and
contribution to a broader academic community, and one of the great joys of my
role in CAM is watching all of you grow in both these regards.

Second, and maybe a little related: humans are messy and unpredictable, and
none of us are mind-readers (which is probably for the best).  We build
understanding by talking to each other — often more than once, since many of us
are highly skilled at missing the point (or forgetting to convey the point).
That’s true in all our personal lives, but also when you’re talking to your
students, your peers, your professors for classes, and your faculty advisors.
So have those conversations and be explicit about your expectations and goals.
It’s sometimes hard, but it really is better to work things out early.
