I have been thinking a lot this past week about uncertainty.  Some of that is
technical (hooray for error analysis!) but some of it is also personal.

We all make decisions in an uncertain world, with uncertain costs and benefits.
It’s not a comfortable position to be in, even if it’s the one that we are
almost always faced with.  Being certain is a lot less work!  But for the best
outcomes, if not for our own comfort, a false certainty is probably a worse
error than living with some extra uncertainty.

I worry about undeserved certainty, both for myself and for the world around
me.  By “undeserved,” I mean uncertainty that has not been eliminated by
careful thought, observation, and study, but by either wishing that it was so
or by trust in an untrustworthy authority.  I don’t know how to proceed through
life without delegating some​ amount of uncertainty reduction to those
that I trust — I just don’t have the resources to know that much on my own, and
I’m not sure I know how to work effectively in a world where I only trust what
I have personally verified end-to-end.  Fortunately, in a society where there
is value and recognition in human expertise, I feel like I can delegate some
uncertainty reduction by the process of finding experts that I have reason to
trust.  Unfortunately, it feels like there is a lot of work underway right now
to reduce the extent to which we value that human expertise.

I only have control over myself and my circumstances, and I’m not sure there’s
much use in staying up late worrying about broad societal trends.  What I can
do, though, is to make sure that I continue to express my own value in
expertise that is out there now, and to invest value in developing the
expertise of future generations.

I am thankful to all of you as you become, I hope, the future experts that I
would like to see in the world.
