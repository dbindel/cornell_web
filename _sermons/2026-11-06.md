It has been an oddly eventful couple weeks in my office.  Let’s make today’s
sermon a collection of things that seemed helpful to tell various people
recently:

- It is a lie to say that there are no bad questions.  But you sometimes find
  the good questions by asking lots of questions and accepting the bad ones (or
  mediocre ones) as part of the deal.
- You can find a lot of errors by reading your paper backward, reading it
  aloud, or copying suspicious text out by hand.  All that is needed is to
  trick your brain into not skipping lightly on to the next thing on your list.
- It is difficult to make predictions, especially about the future (with
  apologies to Yogi Berra).
- Thinking of the most useful way to proceed is often harder than actually
  doing the thing.
- It is not always a sign of progress to add features to code, text to papers,
  and noise to the world.  Carve away what is not beautiful or useful.
- We do what we can to make the world a better place.  Maybe, the world will be
  better for our efforts; certainly, we will be better for our efforts.
