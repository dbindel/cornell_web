For all that it has the reputation of a dry and dusty corner of mathematics,
the literature of numerical analysis has been blessed with some outstanding
writers.  The late J. H. Wilkinson is one such; beyond looking to his papers
for technical insights, I also appreciate the dry humor in his writing, and the
insights that he had beyond mathematics.  Consider, from his 1970 Turing award
lecture “Some comments from a numerical analyst”:

“Turing had a strong predeliction for working things out from first principles,
usually in the first instance without consulting any previous work on the
subject, and no doubt it was this habit which gave his work that
characteristically original flavor.  I was reminded of a remark which Beethoven
is reputed to have made when he was asked if he had heard a certain work of
Mozart which was attracting much attention.  He replied that he had not, and
added “neither shall I do so, lest I forfeit some of my own originality.””

May you all do the work to develop your own characteristically original flavors.
